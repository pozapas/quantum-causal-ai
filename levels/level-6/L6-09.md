## Learn

A classical counterfactual asks what would have occurred under another action for the same relevant background. It requires a structural model that connects observed and alternative worlds. Observational probabilities alone do not generally determine that connection, even before quantum systems are introduced.

Quantum alternatives can involve incompatible measurements and different disturbances. There is no universal joint classical record of all unperformed outcomes. A proposed quantum counterfactual must specify its semantics, available operations, conditioning event, and physical model. Several research approaches make different commitments.

Do not assign simultaneous definite values to all incompatible measurements without an explicit additional model. For an operational question, often an intervention probability is sufficient and better defined. Clearly distinguish a repeatable experimental prediction from a claim about an unperformed event in a particular past trial.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
An operational alternative is p(b \| intervention J, preparation rho). This predicts a new procedure. It is not automatically the same quantity as a same-trial counterfactual conditioned on an incompatible observed outcome.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Prepare \|+&gt; and measure Z, obtaining 0. A new trial with an X measurement gives + with certainty under the preparation model. That operational prediction does not mean the recorded Z trial also possessed a jointly observed X outcome.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Prepare \|0&gt;. In one trial X gives -. State the prediction for Z in a fresh identically prepared trial, and identify the limit of this answer.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

A fresh Z measurement gives 0 with probability one under the preparation model. This predicts a different procedure. It does not supply a joint observed record of incompatible X and Z outcomes in the original trial.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What must accompany a quantum counterfactual claim?**

- **A.** Only an observed frequency
- **B.** A statement that all measurements commute
- **C.** Explicit semantics and a physical model

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. Explicit semantics and a physical model**

Counterfactual claims connect factual and alternative procedures. That connection needs a stated model and semantics beyond one observed frequency table.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
This lesson does not select one universal quantum counterfactual formalism. Treat competing definitions as research choices, not interchangeable formulas.
:::
