## Learn

Causal discovery infers a graph or an equivalence class from data and assumptions. A quantum discovery procedure also needs a specified set of preparations and instruments. Restricted operations can hide distinctions between candidate processes. More samples cannot resolve a distinction absent from the measurement design.

Informationally complete operations span the operator space needed for reconstruction. Their availability is a strong experimental condition. Reconstructed operators also carry statistical error and calibration error. A discovery result should distinguish algebraic identifiability from stable estimation at the available sample size.

Faithfulness rules out certain exact cancellations between mechanisms and observed independences. It is an assumption about the data-generating model. It is not guaranteed by quantum mechanics. Report hidden-system assumptions, dimensional bounds, trusted instruments, and candidate graph restrictions beside the recovered structure.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Two models are observationally indistinguishable for an operation set O when p\_1(a\|o) = p\_2(a\|o) for every allowed o in O and every recorded outcome a.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A device receives only \|0&gt; preparations and is measured only in Z. The identity and Z gates give the same deterministic output. Adding \|+&gt; preparation and X measurement distinguishes them. The added operations improve identifiability; repeating the original setting does not.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

With only \|1&gt; input and Z output measurement, can identity and Z gates be distinguished? Specify one added preparation and measurement that can distinguish them.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

They cannot be distinguished with that restricted design because the phase is global for \|1&gt;. Prepare \|+&gt; and measure X. Identity gives +, while Z gives -.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What can repair a lack of informational access?**

- **A.** Only a larger count at the same uninformative setting
- **B.** A suitable additional preparation or measurement
- **C.** A smaller reported error bar

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. A suitable additional preparation or measurement**

A new informative operation can separate previously identical predictions. Extra repetitions reduce uncertainty but cannot separate exactly equal predictions for the original design.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
No discovery claim is universal across all hidden systems, dimensions, instrument sets, and faithfulness conditions.
:::
