## Learn

A classical common cause can explain correlations through a shared variable. Given its complete value, the local response probabilities factorize in a local hidden-variable model. Bell correlations show that this classical explanation is insufficient under the Bell test assumptions.

A quantum common cause can distribute an entangled state to two laboratories. Each laboratory applies a local measurement. The joint probabilities follow the Born rule on the joint state. They need not admit a factorization over a measurement-independent classical common variable.

This does not require a direct influence between the laboratories. The preparation can be in their shared past. A quantum causal model describes allowed physical systems and transformations along causal connections. It changes the model class rather than granting permission to ignore experimental causal assumptions.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
p(a,b\|x,y) = Tr\[(E\_a\|x tensor F\_b\|y) rho\_AB\]. Here x,y are settings, a,b are outcomes, and E,F are local POVM effects.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A source distributes \|Phi+&gt;. Both laboratories measure Z. They obtain equal outcomes, with 00 and 11 each occurring with probability one half. This table alone also has a classical common-cause explanation. Additional incompatible settings are required to obtain a Bell test.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A source emits the separable mixture of \|00&gt; and \|11&gt; with equal weights. Does perfect Z correlation establish that the source distributed entanglement? Give a reason.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

No. The stated mixture already produces perfect Z correlation without entanglement. One correlation table is insufficient. Use additional settings and a valid entanglement or Bell criterion.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What changes in a quantum common-cause explanation?**

- **A.** The model permits a shared quantum state
- **B.** Probabilities can be negative
- **C.** Every correlation becomes signalling

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. The model permits a shared quantum state**

The shared cause can be a quantum system in an entangled state. Outcome probabilities remain ordinary nonnegative normalized probabilities.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Bell conclusions require the specified locality, setting-independence, and sampling assumptions. Quantum common causes do not remove those assumptions.
:::
