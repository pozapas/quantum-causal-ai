## Learn

Start with a precise question and a list of candidate mechanisms. State which systems enter and leave each laboratory. Separate a direct channel from a common source where the question requires that distinction. List the operations and observations actually available to the experimenter.

Calculate predictions before collecting data. Find settings where the candidates differ, then allocate trials with a declared rule. Include calibration, drift controls, and a method for uncertainty. Reserve independent data for checking a fitted model. Report failures as evidence against the model assumptions.

A good design can return an inconclusive result. Two mechanisms may remain equivalent under restricted operations. In that case, report the equivalence or a bound rather than forcing a single graph. A larger model family needs its own identifiability and statistical analysis.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
For two Bernoulli predictions p and q at one setting, a separation \|p-q\| must be compared with sampling uncertainty and calibration uncertainty. Structural identifiability requires p != q for at least one allowed setting.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Candidate one is an identity channel. Candidate two completely dephases in Z. Preparing \|0&gt; and measuring Z cannot separate them. Preparing \|+&gt; and measuring X gives probability one for + under identity and one half under dephasing. This setting supplies an identifiable comparison.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

For those two candidates, use \|-&gt; preparation and X measurement. Give the probability of outcome - under each model and name one required control.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Identity predicts outcome - with probability one. Complete Z dephasing predicts one half. Calibrate the X measurement or verify preparation stability so apparatus error is not mistaken for channel dephasing.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What is the correct result when all allowed predictions match?**

- **A.** Report an unresolved equivalence under the stated design
- **B.** Choose the simpler graph as proven true
- **C.** Increase decimal precision until they differ

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. Report an unresolved equivalence under the stated design**

Matching predictions imply no identifying evidence within that design. A preference for simplicity can be stated, but it cannot be reported as experimental identification.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
This two-candidate example uses trusted preparations and measurements. General causal discovery can need stronger access and broader candidate models.
:::
