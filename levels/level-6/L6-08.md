## Learn

The quantum switch controls the order in which a target system passes through two operations. One control basis state selects A then B. The other selects B then A. A coherent control can preserve interference between these two ordered alternatives.

A coherent control differs from a classical random choice of order. Losing the control coherence can turn the procedure into a mixture. However, observing an order-dependent outcome alone does not certify causal nonseparability. A trusted witness needs its full operational and mathematical conditions.

Causal nonseparability does not automatically imply a causal-inequality violation. Standard quantum-switch scenarios require careful distinction between these properties. Describe the actual laboratory ports and timing. Do not present the switch as an experiment that sends a message to its own past.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
\|0&gt;\_c\|psi&gt; -&gt; \|0&gt;\_c BA\|psi&gt;; \|1&gt;\_c\|psi&gt; -&gt; \|1&gt;\_c AB\|psi&gt;. For control \|+&gt;, linearity preserves the coherent sum when the implementation retains coherence.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Choose A = X and B = Z. Then ZX = -XZ. With coherent \|+&gt; control, the relative sign between order branches changes the control to \|-&gt;, apart from the common target transformation. A classical order mixture has no such control coherence.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

If A and B are both Z, compare AB and BA. What relative sign is produced by changing their order in this ideal example?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Both products equal Z squared, which is the identity. There is no order-dependent relative sign. The ideal coherent control stays \|+&gt; when no other effect changes it.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which claim follows from the switch construction alone?**

- **A.** It permits a message into the past
- **B.** It coherently controls operation order when coherence is preserved
- **C.** It violates every causal inequality

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. It coherently controls operation order when coherence is preserved**

The construction preserves a coherent sum of ordered alternatives. Claims about witnesses or causal inequalities need additional scenario-specific results and assumptions.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The gate example illustrates coherent order. It is not by itself a complete experimental certification of causal nonseparability.
:::
