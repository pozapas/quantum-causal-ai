## Learn

Bayes' rule connects a conditional probability to its reversed condition. P(flag given defect) is not P(defect given flag). The first describes detector behaviour among defective items. The second asks how many flagged items are actually defective and depends on how common defects are.

Use a large imaginary batch to make the denominators clear. Split the batch into defective and non-defective items first. Then apply the flag rate within each group. Combine both kinds of flags before calculating the fraction that came from defective items.

A low base rate can make false flags numerous even when the false-flag rate is modest. This is arithmetic, not a paradox. A flag is evidence that updates a probability under the model; it is not automatically a certain conclusion about the item.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
P(D \| F)=P(F \| D)P(D)/\[P(F \| D)P(D)+P(F \| not D)P(not D)\], for a positive denominator.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
In a model batch of 1000 items, ten are defective. A detector flags 80 percent of defects, giving eight true flags. It also flags ten percent of 990 sound items, giving 99 false flags. Only 8/107, about 7.48 percent, of flags indicate defects.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A model batch has 1000 items, including 100 defects. A detector flags 90 percent of defects and 20 percent of sound items. What fraction of flags are true defects?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

There are ninety true flags and 180 false flags because 20 percent of 900 is 180. The desired fraction is 90/270=1/3. The detector sensitivity alone is not the answer.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What additional quantity is necessary to reverse a detector's conditional probability?**

- **A.** Only the detector name
- **B.** The colour of the display
- **C.** The base rate of defects

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. The base rate of defects**

The base rate sets the relative sizes of the defective and sound groups. Both groups contribute flags and determine the reversed conditional probability.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
These are fictional manufacturing counts. The model assumes the stated rates apply to this batch.
