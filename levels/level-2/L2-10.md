## Learn

A scientific claim needs a clear meaning and supporting evidence. Record the question, the model, and the data or derivation used to answer it. A title or an impressive diagram is not enough to establish that the claim follows.

Separate a mathematical result from an empirical result. A theorem follows from stated assumptions. An experiment measures a real setup with uncertainty and possible systematic errors. A simulation checks a programmed model and can be wrong because of either model assumptions or implementation errors.

For Quantum Causal AI, ask three separate questions. Is the causal effect identified under the assumptions? Does the quantum model represent the system and data access? Does the learning method improve a specified outcome against suitable alternatives? Evidence for one answer does not establish the others.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Evidence record: claim; assumptions; source; method; result; uncertainty; tested scope.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A simulation shows that a chosen qubit rotation produces a predicted probability curve. This supports agreement between the code and that model over tested inputs. It does not prove that real hardware is noiseless, that a causal effect is identified, or that training is faster.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A report says a quantum predictor has 90 percent accuracy. Name three missing details before judging a claim that it makes better causal decisions.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Ask how new test data were separated from training, which classical baselines were compared, and what evidence identifies the action effect. Also ask about uncertainty, data access, hardware costs, and the exact decision objective.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What does a successful numerical simulation establish most directly?**

- **A.** Universal real-world advantage
- **B.** Agreement with a specified implemented model on tested cases
- **C.** Every causal assumption is true

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. Agreement with a specified implemented model on tested cases**

A simulation tests computations within an implemented model. Claims about real systems, causal identification, or advantage require further evidence beyond numerical agreement.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
No finite test establishes every future use. Keep claims inside the scope of the evidence.
:::
