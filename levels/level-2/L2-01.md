## Learn

A variable is a named quantity whose value can change. Let x be the number of tickets purchased. Let y be the total price in dollars. Naming the quantities and their units prevents confusion when an equation is used.

An equation states equality between two expressions. If each ticket costs three dollars and there is a two-dollar fee, write y=3x+2. The coefficient three gives the price per ticket, and the constant two gives the fixed fee.

An equation can describe an observed pattern without being a causal law. A fitted line connecting two measurements does not prove that changing the first will change the second. Its interpretation depends on the mechanism and assumptions behind the model.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
y = 3x + 2, where x is ticket count and y is total price in dollars.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
For four tickets, substitute x=4 into y=3x+2. First multiply three by four to get twelve. Then add the fixed fee of two. The total is fourteen dollars under this stated pricing rule.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A service costs five dollars per item plus a fixed fee of four dollars. Write the rule and find the price for three items.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Let x be item count and y be price in dollars. The rule is y=5x+4. For three items, y=15+4=19 dollars. The fixed fee is added once.
:::

## Check your understanding

**In y=5x+4, what does the four represent in the stated price model?**

- **A.** The item count
- **B.** The fixed fee
- **C.** The cost of every item

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. The fixed fee**

The constant is added once regardless of item count. The coefficient five multiplies the count and represents the per-item cost.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
A numerical relationship needs an explicit interpretation before it can support a causal claim.
:::
