## Learn

A vector is an ordered list of numbers. The order matters because each entry has a stated meaning. For two recorded outcomes zero and one, the vector (0.3,0.7) can list their probabilities in that order.

A classical probability vector has nonnegative entries that add to one. This is normalization. A quantum state vector instead lists amplitudes in a chosen basis. Its squared amplitude magnitudes add to one, and relative phase can affect predictions in other measurements.

Do not switch these normalization rules. The amplitude vector (1 divided by square root of two, 1 divided by square root of two) gives equal measurement probabilities after squaring magnitudes. The amplitude sum itself is not required to equal one.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Classical: p0+p1=1. Pure qubit: \|a0\|^2+\|a1\|^2=1. Here p are probabilities and a are amplitudes.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
The probability vector (0.2,0.8) is normalized because both entries are nonnegative and their sum is one. The real amplitude vector (0.6,0.8) is normalized under the quantum rule because 0.6 squared plus 0.8 squared equals 0.36+0.64=1.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Check whether (0.4,0.6) is a probability vector. Then use the normalized real amplitude vector (0,1) to find computational-basis outcome probabilities.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The first vector is a probability vector because its nonnegative entries sum to one. For amplitudes (0,1), squared magnitudes give probabilities (0,1), so outcome one is certain in that basis.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which rule normalizes a pure quantum state vector?**

- **A.** Squared magnitudes of amplitudes sum to one
- **B.** Amplitudes must be positive integers
- **C.** The raw amplitude sum is always one

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. Squared magnitudes of amplitudes sum to one**

Quantum measurement probabilities are squared magnitudes. Their total must be one, while amplitudes themselves can be negative or complex and need not sum to one.
:::
