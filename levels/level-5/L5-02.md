## Learn

A closed quantum system evolves through a unitary matrix. A matrix U is unitary when its conjugate transpose times U is the identity. This property preserves vector length and total probability. A circuit records an ordered sequence of such operations.

The X gate exchanges \|0&gt; and \|1&gt;. The Hadamard gate H makes equal superpositions from these basis states. The Z gate changes the sign of the \|1&gt; amplitude. These changes affect later measurements even when current probabilities stay unchanged.

With column vectors, the rightmost matrix acts first. Circuit drawings usually show time from left to right. State this convention before multiplying gates. Measurement and noise are generally not unitary operations on the measured system alone.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
H = (1/sqrt(2)) \[\[1,1\],\[1,-1\]\]; Z = diag(1,-1); HZH = X. Matrices act on column state vectors.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Start in \|0&gt;. Apply H to get (\|0&gt; + \|1&gt;)/sqrt(2). Apply Z to get (\|0&gt; - \|1&gt;)/sqrt(2). Apply H again to get \|1&gt;. A final computational-basis measurement therefore gives 1 with probability one in the ideal circuit.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Start in \|1&gt;. Apply H, then Z, then H. Calculate the final state and its computational-basis outcome.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The combined operation is HZH = X. The X gate maps \|1&gt; to \|0&gt;. The final state is \|0&gt;, so the outcome is 0 with probability one.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which matrix acts first in U V \|psi&gt;?**

- **A.** U
- **B.** Both at once
- **C.** V

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. V**

For column vectors, evaluate the expression from the right. V first changes the state, and U acts on that result.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Ideal gates describe a noiseless model. Hardware operations can differ from their target matrices.
:::
