## Learn

The von Neumann entropy measures mixedness through the eigenvalues of a density operator. Use logarithm base two to express entropy in bits. A pure state has zero entropy. A maximally mixed qubit has one bit of entropy.

Quantum tomography estimates a state from measurements of many equally prepared systems. For a qubit, measurements of X, Y, and Z determine a Bloch vector. A physical Bloch vector has length at most one. Statistical estimates can violate this condition before constrained fitting.

A full density matrix grows rapidly with system size. Tomography can therefore require many settings and samples. State assumptions can reduce this cost but also restrict the conclusion. A neural estimator does not remove the need for an identifiable measurement design.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
S(rho) = -sum\_j lambda\_j log2(lambda\_j); rho = (I + rx X + ry Y + rz Z)/2. The lambda\_j are eigenvalues and r is the Bloch vector.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A qubit has measured means rx = 0, ry = 0, rz = 0.6. Its eigenvalues are (1+0.6)/2 = 0.8 and 0.2. Its entropy is approximately 0.722 bits. These means identify a diagonal mixed state under the trusted measurement model.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A fitted Bloch vector is (0,0,1). Give the eigenvalues and entropy. Explain whether additional samples can expose arbitrary global phase.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The eigenvalues are one and zero, so the entropy is zero. This is a pure state. No number of samples can reveal a physically irrelevant global phase.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which three means specify a qubit density operator?**

- **A.** X, Y, and Z expectation values
- **B.** Only three repeated Z outcomes
- **C.** Only its entropy

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. X, Y, and Z expectation values**

The three Pauli means specify the Bloch vector. Entropy or one measurement basis alone does not determine all state components.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Tomography assumes a stable preparation and a calibrated measurement model. These assumptions must be checked separately from the fit.
:::
