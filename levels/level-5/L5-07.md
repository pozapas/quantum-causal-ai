## Learn

A positive operator valued measure, or POVM, specifies outcome probabilities. Each effect is positive and the effects sum to the identity. The probability is the trace of an effect times the state. Effects alone do not determine the state after measurement.

A quantum instrument adds the state change for each recorded outcome. Each outcome map is completely positive and trace nonincreasing. Their sum is a channel. Divide the outcome map result by its trace to obtain the normalized conditional state.

This distinction matters for causal experiments. Two apparatuses can have the same immediate outcome probabilities but prepare different states for the next laboratory. An experiment that records only outcomes can miss this difference. Include the operational intervention when specifying the model.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
p(a) = Tr(E\_a rho); rho\_a = I\_a(rho)/p(a). Here E\_a is a POVM effect, I\_a is its instrument map, and p(a)&gt;0.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A Z measurement of \|+&gt; gives outcome 0 with probability 1/2. A projective instrument then leaves \|0&gt;. Another instrument can record the same outcome and prepare \|1&gt;. The first probabilities match, but a later Z measurement distinguishes the two apparatuses.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A device measures Z, records the outcome, then always prepares \|+&gt;. What is the probability of 1 in a later Z measurement?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The final preparation is \|+&gt;, independent of the first outcome. A later Z measurement therefore gives 1 with probability one half. The first measurement effect alone would not specify this result.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What extra information does an instrument supply beyond a POVM?**

- **A.** The conditional state change
- **B.** A different probability rule
- **C.** A unique hidden variable

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. The conditional state change**

A POVM gives the probabilities of recorded outcomes. An instrument also specifies the physical transformation associated with each recorded outcome.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Conditioning on an outcome with zero probability is undefined. State this limit when writing conditional-state formulas.
:::
