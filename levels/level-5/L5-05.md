## Learn

A density operator describes both pure states and statistical mixtures. It is positive semidefinite and has trace one. For a pure state, form the outer product \|psi&gt;&lt;psi\|. A mixture is a weighted sum of these outer products.

Purity is the trace of the squared density operator. A pure state has purity one. A mixed qubit can have purity as low as one half. Low local purity can arise from classical uncertainty or from entanglement with another system.

The partial trace gives local predictions from a joint state. It is an information reduction, not an assumption that the other system was physically destroyed. Two different joint states can have the same reduced state. Local data then cannot distinguish those joint preparations.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
rho = sum\_j p\_j \|psi\_j&gt;&lt;psi\_j\|; purity = Tr(rho²); rho\_A = Tr\_B(rho\_AB). The weights p\_j are nonnegative and sum to one.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Take rho = diag(0.8,0.2). Its square has diagonal entries 0.64 and 0.04. The purity is 0.68. This is a valid mixed qubit state. Its Z-basis probabilities are 0.8 and 0.2, but this diagonal form contains no phase coherence.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Find the purity of diag(0.6,0.4). Explain whether this operator describes a pure qubit state.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Square both diagonal entries and add them. The result is 0.36 + 0.16 = 0.52. Since this is below one, the state is mixed.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What does a reduced density operator determine?**

- **A.** Every joint correlation
- **B.** Only the global preparation label
- **C.** All measurement probabilities on that subsystem

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. All measurement probabilities on that subsystem**

The reduced operator determines local probabilities. It does not retain the full joint correlation structure or uniquely identify the global preparation.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
A mixed-state decomposition is generally not unique. Do not interpret one decomposition as a uniquely observed hidden preparation.
:::
