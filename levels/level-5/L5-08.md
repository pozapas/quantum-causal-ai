## Learn

A Bell experiment compares correlations under several local measurement choices. In the CHSH test, each laboratory chooses between two settings and records a sign. A local hidden-variable model obeys an absolute CHSH bound of two under its measurement-independence and locality assumptions.

Quantum theory permits values up to two times the square root of two. This exceeds the local bound but still respects no signalling. Neither outcome lets one laboratory choose the other outcome. A violation excludes a stated class of models, rather than every possible causal explanation.

Real tests must address detection, setting selection, timing, and statistical uncertainty. Selecting only convenient detected events can invalidate the comparison. Finite samples require a defined test statistic and uncertainty method. A simulated expected value is not an experimental loophole-free Bell test.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
S = E00 + E01 + E10 - E11; \|S\| &lt;= 2 locally; \|S\| &lt;= 2sqrt(2) quantum mechanically. Exy is the mean product of signs for settings x,y.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Suppose the four correlations are 1/sqrt(2), 1/sqrt(2), 1/sqrt(2), and -1/sqrt(2). Their signed sum is 4/sqrt(2), or about 2.828. Uniform local marginals can accompany these correlations. Thus a Bell violation does not imply a signalling channel.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

For correlations 0.6,0.6,0.6,-0.6, calculate S. Compare it with the local bound without making a statistical significance claim.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The signed sum is 0.6 + 0.6 + 0.6 - (-0.6) = 2.4. This exceeds two as a point value. Sample counts and a statistical test are still needed.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Does a CHSH violation permit a controllable faster-than-light message?**

- **A.** Yes, whenever S exceeds two
- **B.** Only for pure states
- **C.** No, quantum marginals can obey no signalling

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. No, quantum marginals can obey no signalling**

The violation concerns local hidden-variable explanations. Quantum joint correlations can violate that bound while each local marginal stays independent of the remote setting.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The local bound assumes suitable locality and setting independence. Experimental confidence requires a valid finite-sample analysis.
:::
