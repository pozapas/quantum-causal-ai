## Learn

A vector is an ordered list of numbers. In this lesson, its entries can be complex. The order matters because each position names a basis direction. A two-entry vector can describe an arrow in a plane, or the amplitudes of a two-outcome quantum system.

The inner product compares two vectors. Conjugate the entries of the first vector, multiply corresponding entries, then add. The inner product of a vector with itself is its squared norm. Divide a nonzero vector by its norm to obtain a normalized vector.

Orthogonal vectors have inner product zero. For a normalized quantum state, the squared magnitude of its inner product with a normalized measurement basis vector gives that outcome's probability. This statement requires an orthonormal measurement basis. An arbitrary coordinate entry is not automatically a physical probability.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
⟨u,v⟩ = Σj u*j vj; \|\|v\|\| = √⟨v,v⟩. The index j names an entry, and \* means complex conjugation.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Take v = (3,4). Its norm is √(9 + 16) = 5. The normalized vector is (3/5,4/5). Its overlap with e0 = (1,0) is 3/5. If these are quantum amplitudes in the stated basis, outcome zero has probability 9/25.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Normalize v = (1,1). Calculate the squared overlap with e1 = (0,1).
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The norm is √2. The normalized vector is (1/√2,1/√2). Its overlap with e1 is 1/√2, and the squared overlap is 1/2.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What does an inner product of zero mean?**

- **A.** The vectors have equal length
- **B.** The vectors must both be zero
- **C.** The vectors are orthogonal

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. The vectors are orthogonal**

Orthogonality is defined by a zero inner product. Nonzero vectors can be orthogonal even when their lengths differ.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
An inner product is a mathematical overlap. Its physical interpretation depends on the model and measurement.
:::
