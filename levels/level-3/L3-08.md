## Learn

Entropy measures uncertainty in a probability distribution. It does not measure the size or importance of an outcome. A fair coin has more entropy than a coin that always gives heads. The logarithm base determines the information unit. Base two gives bits.

For a discrete distribution, multiply each probability by the negative logarithm of that probability, then add. A zero-probability term contributes zero by continuity. For two possible outcomes, entropy is largest when their probabilities are equal. It becomes zero when either outcome is certain.

Classical entropy applies to recorded outcomes. Quantum states use a related quantity called von Neumann entropy, which depends on the density matrix eigenvalues. A pure quantum state can have zero von Neumann entropy while a chosen measurement on that state still has uncertain outcomes.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
H(X) = -Σx p(x)log₂p(x), with 0log₂0 defined as 0. Here p(x) is the probability of outcome x and H is measured in bits.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A fair binary outcome has p(0) = p(1) = 1/2. Each logarithm is -1. Thus H = -(1/2)(-1) -(1/2)(-1) = 1 bit. A certain zero has entropy zero because the only nonzero probability is one, whose logarithm is zero.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A device produces one of four equally likely symbols. Calculate its entropy in bits.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Each symbol has probability 1/4 and logarithm -2. Four equal contributions of -(1/4)(-2) give an entropy of 2 bits per symbol.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which binary distribution has the largest entropy?**

- **A.** Probabilities 0.5 and 0.5
- **B.** Probabilities 0.9 and 0.1
- **C.** Probabilities 1 and 0

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. Probabilities 0.5 and 0.5**

Equal binary probabilities give maximum uncertainty over two outcomes. Increasing certainty about either outcome reduces the binary entropy.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Entropy depends on the stated distribution or quantum state. Measurement entropy and state entropy need not be equal.
:::
