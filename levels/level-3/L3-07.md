## Learn

A probability distribution assigns probabilities to possible outcomes. Its expectation is the probability-weighted average. For a finite set, multiply each outcome by its probability, then add. The probabilities must be nonnegative and sum to one. A mean can differ from every individual outcome.

Variance measures the average squared distance from the expectation. Squaring prevents positive and negative deviations from canceling. The standard deviation is its square root. Variance has squared outcome units. The standard deviation has the same units as the original measured outcome.

Repeated observations help estimate these quantities. Independent observations have a simpler error calculation than dependent observations. If measurements share drift or another cause, more samples do not necessarily reduce error as quickly as the independent formula predicts. Record the sampling assumptions before reporting uncertainty.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
E\[X\] = Σx xP(X=x); Var(X) = E\[(X-E\[X\])²\] = E\[X²\]-E\[X\]². Here X is a random variable.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Let X be one with probability 0.25 and zero with probability 0.75. The mean is 0.25. Since X² = X, its second moment is also 0.25. The variance is 0.25 - 0.25² = 0.1875. Most single trials still return zero.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Let X take values -1 and 1 with equal probability. Find its expectation and variance.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The expectation is (-1)(1/2) + (1)(1/2) = 0. Both squared outcomes equal one. Thus E\[X²\] = 1 and the variance is 1 - 0² = 1.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which quantity always uses squared outcome units?**

- **A.** The expectation
- **B.** The standard deviation
- **C.** The variance

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. The variance**

Variance averages squared deviations. Taking its square root produces standard deviation, which returns to the original outcome units.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The usual standard error of a sample mean assumes independent observations with a common finite variance.
:::
