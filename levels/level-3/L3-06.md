## Learn

A derivative describes the local rate of change of a function. Approximate it by a difference quotient over a small interval. The derivative is the limit as that interval approaches zero, when the limit exists. It is not generally a constant over a large interval.

A gradient collects partial derivatives for a function with several inputs. Each partial derivative varies one input while holding the other coordinates fixed. Optimization algorithms use this local information to choose a direction. The units of a derivative depend on the units of its output and input.

A derivative inside a fitted prediction function is not automatically a causal effect. Holding model coordinates fixed is a mathematical operation. A physical intervention can change other variables through causal mechanisms. To interpret a derivative causally, specify the causal model and the intervention that the derivative represents.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
f′(x) = lim\[h→0\](f(x+h)-f(x))/h. For f(x,y), ∇f = (∂f/∂x,∂f/∂y). The variable h is the input change.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Let f(x) = x². The quotient is ((x+h)²-x²)/h = 2x+h. Its limit is 2x. At x = 3, the derivative is 6. A small positive step therefore increases the output by about six times the step size.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

For g(x,y) = x² + 3y², find the gradient at x = 2 and y = -1.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The partial derivatives are 2x and 6y. Substitute the point to obtain the gradient (4,-6). This vector gives local change in the two coordinate directions.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Does a nonzero regression derivative by itself prove a causal effect?**

- **A.** Yes, for every fitted function
- **B.** No, it needs causal assumptions
- **C.** Yes, if the derivative is large

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. No, it needs causal assumptions**

A prediction function can reflect confounding or selection. A causal interpretation needs a justified model of interventions and mechanisms.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Differentiation describes mathematical change. Use separate assumptions to connect that change to a causal intervention.
:::
