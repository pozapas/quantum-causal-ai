## Learn

An estimator is a rule that maps data to an estimate. A sample proportion estimates the probability of a binary outcome. It varies between repeated samples. This sampling variation differs from model bias, instrument error, and a change in the population over time.

A confidence interval is produced by a procedure with a stated long-run coverage under its assumptions. A 95 percent procedure covers the fixed parameter in about 95 percent of repeated samples. It does not assign a 95 percent frequentist probability to the parameter after observing one interval.

Testing many hypotheses increases opportunities for false findings. Plan the analysis before inspecting outcomes when possible. Report the number of tested claims and use an appropriate correction or independent confirmation. Statistical significance cannot repair confounding, a wrong measurement model, or an undefined causal target.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
p̂ = k/n; SE(p̂) ≈ √\[p̂(1-p̂)/n\] for independent Bernoulli trials. Here k is the success count and n is the trial count.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Observe 60 successes in 100 independent binary trials. The estimate is 0.60. The approximate standard error is √(0.6×0.4/100), about 0.049. This calculation describes sampling variation. It does not include systematic errors such as a detector that misses the same type of event.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Observe 100 successes in 400 independent binary trials. Find the estimated probability and approximate standard error.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The estimate is 100/400 = 0.25. The approximate standard error is √(0.25×0.75/400), about 0.0217. Independence and a stable binary probability are required for this formula.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What can a larger independent sample usually reduce?**

- **A.** All systematic bias
- **B.** Every form of confounding
- **C.** Sampling variation of an estimate

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. Sampling variation of an estimate**

More independent observations can reduce sampling variation. Systematic measurement errors and causal confounding need separate design or modeling solutions.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The simple normal approximation can fail near probability zero or one or with very small sample counts.
:::
