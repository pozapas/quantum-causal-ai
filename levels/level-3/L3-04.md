## Learn

The tensor product builds a joint space from separate spaces. For two two-entry vectors, it produces four entries. Fix the order as 00, 01, 10, 11. Each entry is a product of one entry from the first vector and one from the second.

A product vector factors into one vector for each subsystem. A general joint quantum vector need not have this form. A pure state that cannot factor across a stated partition is entangled. This definition concerns joint amplitudes, rather than merely the presence of matching measurement results.

For classical independent variables, joint probabilities also form a product. Correlated classical variables do not. Thus a four-entry list alone does not tell you whether a model is classical or quantum. State what the entries mean and what operations the model permits before drawing a physical conclusion.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
(a,b) ⊗ (c,d) = (ac,ad,bc,bd), in the basis order 00,01,10,11. The symbol ⊗ means tensor product.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Take the first state as (1,0) and the second as (1/√2,1/√2). Their joint vector is (1/√2,1/√2,0,0). The first subsystem always gives zero in this basis. The second subsystem gives either outcome with probability one half. This state is a product state.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Find (0,1) ⊗ (1,0). Which joint basis outcome has probability one?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The tensor product is (0,0,1,0). The nonzero entry is in position 10, so the first subsystem gives one and the second gives zero.
:::

## Check your understanding

**How many amplitudes describe a general pure state of three qubits?**

- **A.** Three
- **B.** Six
- **C.** Eight

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. Eight**

Each added qubit doubles the joint basis size. Three qubits therefore use 2³ = 8 complex amplitudes before normalization constraints.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Exponential state-vector size does not prove an exponential advantage for every quantum computation.
:::
