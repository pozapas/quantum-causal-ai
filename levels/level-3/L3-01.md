## Learn

A complex number has a real part and an imaginary part. Write it as z = a + ib. The numbers a and b are real. The symbol i satisfies i squared = -1. Plot a on the horizontal axis and b on the vertical axis.

The complex conjugate changes the sign of the imaginary part. For z = a + ib, its conjugate is a - ib. Multiply the two numbers. The imaginary terms cancel. The result is a squared plus b squared. Its square root is the magnitude.

Phase is the angle of the point from the positive real axis. Two numbers can have equal magnitude and different phases. Quantum amplitudes use both properties. Probability depends on squared magnitude, but a later operation can turn a phase difference into a probability difference.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
z = a + ib; z* = a - ib; \|z\|² = z*z = a² + b². Here z* is the complex conjugate and \|z\| is the magnitude.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Let z = 3 + 4i. Its conjugate is 3 - 4i. Their product is 9 + 16 = 25. Thus \|z\| = 5. The number -3 + 4i also has magnitude 5, but it points in a different direction.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Find the squared magnitude of z = 1 - 2i. Does z have the same phase as 1 + 2i?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The squared magnitude is 1² + (-2)² = 5. The two points lie on opposite sides of the real axis, so their phases differ.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which operation produces the squared magnitude of z?**

- **A.** Add z to its conjugate
- **B.** Multiply z by its conjugate
- **C.** Multiply z by i

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. Multiply z by its conjugate**

The product with the conjugate is a² + b². It is real and cannot be negative.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
A complex number is a mathematical object. It becomes a quantum amplitude only within a specified quantum model.
