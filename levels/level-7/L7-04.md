## Learn

A barren plateau is a regime where gradients concentrate near zero as problem size grows under stated circuit and cost assumptions. This is a property of a landscape distribution. One failed optimization run is not enough to establish that scaling behavior.

To test trainability, vary system size while controlling the circuit family, initialization, observable, and noise model. Estimate gradient variance across independent initializations. Compare that variance with shot noise. A flat-looking estimated landscape can result from limited measurement precision rather than a vanishing mathematical gradient.

Proposed remedies include structured circuits, local costs, informed initialization, and staged training. Their value depends on the task and assumptions. A remedy should preserve expressive power and generalization while improving measured scaling. Do not call one successful small circuit a general solution.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
For N independent sign measurements, Var(mean) = (1-f²)/N &lt;= 1/N. For two independent shifted means with N shots each, Var(gradient estimate) &lt;= 1/(2N).
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A two-shift gradient estimate uses 500 shots at each shift. Its variance is at most 1/1000, so its standard deviation is at most about 0.0316. A true gradient near 0.001 can be hidden by this noise. More precise estimation can be costly.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Use 2000 shots at each shift. Compute the worst-case gradient standard deviation and explain whether this calculation proves a barren plateau.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The variance bound is 1/4000. Its square root is about 0.0158. This bounds estimator noise only. A barren-plateau claim requires controlled gradient scaling across circuit sizes and initializations.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which evidence supports a scaling claim about barren plateaus?**

- **A.** Controlled gradient statistics across increasing sizes
- **B.** One optimizer run with no improvement
- **C.** One large error bar

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. Controlled gradient statistics across increasing sizes**

A scaling claim needs repeated gradient statistics under controlled circuit assumptions at several sizes. A single optimization failure has many alternative explanations.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The original barren-plateau result applies to specified circuit ensembles. It does not state that every useful quantum model is untrainable.
:::
