## Learn

A benchmark needs an explicit data generator or physical source, train and test splits, metrics, and resource limits. For causal tasks, reserve intervention tests that differ from training conditions. Predictive agreement on the original settings does not by itself measure intervention recovery.

Compare with strong classical models, simple physical baselines, and ablations. An ablation removes a selected feature to test its contribution. Match tuning budgets and prevent test-set feedback during model selection. Use several seeds or repeat experiments to quantify variation.

Record the exact circuit, device calibration, software version, shot allocation, and stopping rule. Save raw counts where permitted and enough metadata to reconstruct the analysis. Separate simulator results from hardware results. A simulation validates code and model calculations, not an advantage on a physical processor.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Report prediction error on held-out settings, target error \|T\_hat-T\| where a reference is known, interval coverage across repeats, and total counted resources. Do not collapse these different quantities into one unexplained score.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Train identity and dephasing candidates on Z-basis data only. Both fit equally well. Test with \|+&gt; preparation and X measurement. A benchmark that includes only the original Z settings would reward both and fail to test the claimed coherence mechanism.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Propose one ablation and one held-out test for a learner that claims to use intervention information in quantum channel identification.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Remove intervention-setting labels or restrict the training operation set in the ablation. Test on a distinct informative preparation and measurement pair. Compare target error and uncertainty under equal resource budgets.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which split tests intervention recovery more directly?**

- **A.** Another copy of the same training records
- **B.** Held-out informative intervention settings
- **C.** A larger training score display

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. Held-out informative intervention settings**

New informative interventions test the claimed mechanism beyond the original observations. Reusing the same setting distribution can leave causal ambiguity unchanged.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Synthetic ground truth validates performance for that generator. It does not establish that the same causal assumptions hold in another physical system.
:::
