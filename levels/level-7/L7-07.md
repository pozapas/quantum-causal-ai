## Learn

A classical shadow stores randomized measurement data in a form that estimates many observables. Each measurement setting and outcome defines an inverted snapshot. Averaging snapshots gives an estimator for selected properties. The snapshot need not itself be a physical density operator.

Efficiency depends on the measurement ensemble and the target observables. For local random Pauli measurements, a high-weight observable can have a large shadow norm. A logarithmic dependence on the number of requested observables does not mean every observable can be estimated with a system-size-independent constant cost.

Use a measurement ensemble suited to the property family. State the error and confidence target and account for reconstruction time. Classical shadows do not provide a free complete state description at arbitrary accuracy. They also do not remove causal identification assumptions when the target is an intervention property.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
For one qubit with a uniformly random X,Y,Z basis and observed eigenstate \|b&gt;, a snapshot is rho\_hat = 3\|b&gt;&lt;b\| - I. Its ensemble average equals rho under ideal calibrated measurements.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
For a Z-basis outcome 0, the snapshot is diag(2,-1). Its trace is one, but one eigenvalue is negative. This is valid as an estimator. Averaging over the randomized experiment, rather than interpreting each snapshot as a prepared state, recovers the density operator.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Write the snapshot for a Z-basis outcome 1. Give its trace and explain why it need not be positive.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The snapshot is diag(-1,2), with trace one. It is an inverted statistical estimator, so positivity is not required for one snapshot. The ensemble mean reconstructs the physical state.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What controls the measurement cost beyond the observable count?**

- **A.** The measurement ensemble and observable-dependent shadow norm
- **B.** Only the number of stored files
- **C.** Only the Hilbert-space label

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. The measurement ensemble and observable-dependent shadow norm**

Observable structure and measurement choice control estimator variance. The logarithmic observable-count factor does not remove these other potentially large factors.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The simple snapshot formula assumes ideal uniform local Pauli measurements. Noise and different ensembles require a calibrated inverse or another estimator.
:::
