## Learn

A variational circuit contains adjustable gate parameters. A classical optimizer updates them using an estimated loss or gradient. The circuit expectation is a smooth mathematical function in the ideal model. Hardware estimates add finite-sample uncertainty and possible device bias.

For one gate exp(-i theta P/2), with P squared equal to identity, an expectation has only the relevant sine and cosine dependence on that parameter. Two shifted circuit evaluations therefore give its exact derivative. This is an analytic identity, not a small-step finite difference.

The simple two-shift rule assumes one occurrence of the parameter in the stated gate form. A shared parameter at several gates requires summing gate contributions or a suitable generalized rule. A nonlinear loss needs the chain rule. Noisy estimates still carry measurement variance.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
For f(theta)=&lt;O&gt; after one exp(-i theta P/2) gate with P²=I: f prime(theta) = \[f(theta+pi/2)-f(theta-pi/2)\]/2. Other circuit parameters are held fixed.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Prepare \|0&gt;, apply Ry(theta), and measure Z. Then f(theta)=cos(theta). At theta=pi/6, the shifted values are -1/2 and 1/2. Half their difference is -1/2. This equals the analytic derivative -sin(pi/6). The agreement checks both sign and scale in the shift formula.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

For the same circuit at theta=pi/3, calculate the exact gradient. If loss is (f-y)², write its derivative using f prime.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The gradient is -sqrt(3)/2. The squared-loss derivative is 2(f-y) f prime. The parameter-shift rule supplies f prime; the loss itself also needs the classical chain rule.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What is the basic two-shift rule for the stated gate?**

- **A.** A finite difference with an arbitrary tiny step
- **B.** An exact expectation derivative under its generator assumptions
- **C.** A zero-variance hardware estimator

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. An exact expectation derivative under its generator assumptions**

The fixed shifts follow from the two-eigenvalue generator. The identity is exact for expectations, while finite-shot estimates still have uncertainty.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Generators with other spectra or repeated parameter use need a different or expanded rule. Do not apply the basic formula without checking those conditions.
:::
