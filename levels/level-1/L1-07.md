## Learn

A wave describes a change that extends across space or time. A water surface can have crests and troughs. A crest is a high point, and a trough is a low point relative to the resting surface.

Amplitude measures the size of the disturbance from its resting value. Wavelength is the distance between repeating points, such as adjacent crests. Phase describes where a wave is within its repeated cycle. Two equal waves can have different phases.

A drawing of a water wave is a useful first model of combination. It is not a drawing of a qubit. Quantum amplitudes have their own rules and need not describe a physical water height or a particle moving in a visible loop.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
One full cycle has phase 360 degrees. Half a cycle has phase 180 degrees.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Two water-wave drawings have crests four centimetres apart. Each has wavelength four centimetres. One drawing is shifted by two centimetres, so its crest matches the other trough. The shift is half a wavelength, or half a cycle.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A wave has crests at positions 1, 7, and 13 centimetres. Find the wavelength. What distance is half a wavelength?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The distance between adjacent crests is six centimetres, so the wavelength is six centimetres. Half a wavelength is three centimetres. Use adjacent matching points, not crest height, for this distance.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What does a half-cycle phase shift align for equal sinusoidal waves?**

- **A.** Every crest with another crest
- **B.** No points at all
- **C.** A crest with the other wave's trough

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. A crest with the other wave's trough**

A half cycle moves the wave from a crest to a trough. Equal wave heights then have opposite signs at corresponding positions.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The wave picture is an analogy. Quantum state amplitudes are not water heights.
:::
