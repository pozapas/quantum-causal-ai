## Learn

A coin under a cup can have a fixed face that you do not know. Your uncertainty is about missing information. A probability model can describe that uncertainty without saying that the coin follows quantum information rules in this task.

A qubit state can give the same measurement probabilities as a classical random bit for one chosen measurement. That agreement does not make the models identical. Other measurement choices or controlled operations can expose different predictions, including effects of relative phase.

Do not picture a qubit as merely a secret ordinary bit. Also do not claim that one measurement reveals its whole state. State learning requires suitable preparations and many measurement results. A model is tested through its predictions across allowed experiments.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Same test probabilities can hide different states. Different tests can distinguish their predictions.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
The quantum states called plus and minus both give zero or one with equal probabilities in the usual computational-basis measurement. A different basis can distinguish them perfectly in the ideal model. Their relative phases differ even though the first probabilities match.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Two devices each give half zeros in one test. Can that result alone prove that they prepare the same quantum state? Explain what is missing.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

No. One test supplies only limited probabilities. Different quantum states can share those probabilities. Additional suitable measurement settings and repeated preparations are necessary to compare their other predictions.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Why is an unknown classical bit not a full model of a qubit?**

- **A.** Classical bits cannot be recorded
- **B.** Qubit predictions also depend on phase and measurement basis
- **C.** Every qubit gives a certain answer

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. Qubit predictions also depend on phase and measurement basis**

Quantum states include phase relations that affect results after suitable operations or measurements. An unknown fixed bit does not supply those quantum prediction rules.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
This distinction is operational. It does not settle philosophical interpretations of quantum theory.
:::
