## Learn

A common cause is a factor that can affect two other factors. Hot weather can increase ice cream sales and swimming visits. The two activities can occur together because both respond to the weather, even without either causing the other.

A causal diagram uses arrows to state proposed direct influences. Draw weather pointing to sales and weather pointing to swimming. These arrows express a model. They need scientific support and are not created merely by drawing lines between recorded quantities.

A shared cause can make a prediction useful while making an action claim wrong. If sales predict swimming, increasing sales alone might still fail to increase swimming. The action removes the usual reason that high sales were observed.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Shared-cause diagram: sales &lt;- weather -&gt; swimming. An arrow points from proposed cause to effect.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
On ten warm days, a pool sells many tickets and a shop sells many cold drinks. On cool days both totals fall. Warm weather is a plausible shared cause. The record alone does not show that selling a drink sends someone to the pool.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Roads are wet and umbrellas are common. Propose a common cause. What extra question would you ask before saying umbrellas make roads wet?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Rain is a plausible common cause of wet roads and umbrella use. Ask whether deliberately opening umbrellas without rain changes road wetness under a suitable controlled comparison.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which diagram represents Z as a common cause of X and Y?**

- **A.** X -&gt; Z &lt;- Y
- **B.** X &lt;- Z -&gt; Y
- **C.** X -&gt; Y -&gt; Z

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. X &lt;- Z -&gt; Y**

Both arrows leave Z and enter the other variables. The diagram therefore states that Z directly influences X and Y in this model.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
A plausible common cause is a hypothesis. The diagram requires evidence and stated assumptions.
:::
