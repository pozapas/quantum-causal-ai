## Learn

A relative frequency is the event count divided by the number of trials. It is a measured result. A probability belongs to a model. The measured frequency can differ from the model probability even when the model is suitable.

To keep bag trials comparable, replace the drawn counter after each trial and mix again. Without replacement, the bag changes. If you draw a red counter and keep it out, later red draws can become less likely.

Many independent trials can make large frequency errors less likely. They do not force an exact balance in every small block of trials. A fair coin does not need to produce a tail because several heads occurred before it.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
relative frequency = event count / trial count. A trial count must be positive.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A fair-coin model gives P(heads)=0.5. In ten tosses you record seven heads, so the relative frequency is 7/10=0.7. This short record differs from the model value. It does not by itself prove that the coin model is wrong.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A simulation records eighteen blue draws in sixty independent replacement trials. Compute the blue relative frequency. Does it determine the next draw?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The relative frequency is 18/60=0.3. It summarizes these sixty results. It does not determine the next draw, whose probability depends on the trial model and unchanged draw conditions.
:::

## Check your understanding

**A fair coin gives four heads in a row. What is P(heads) on the next independent toss?**

- **A.** Zero
- **B.** One half
- **C.** One

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. One half**

Independence means the earlier tosses do not change the next toss probability. The fair-coin model still assigns one half to heads.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Real trials can be dependent. Independence must be justified by the setup.
:::
