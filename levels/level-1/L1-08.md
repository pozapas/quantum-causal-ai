## Learn

When two simple wave disturbances meet, their signed heights can add. Two upward disturbances can make a larger upward disturbance. An upward and an equal downward disturbance can cancel at one point. The phase relation decides how they combine.

Quantum interference also depends on combining amplitudes before finding probabilities. An amplitude is a mathematical quantity used by the quantum rules. It can include a phase. The probability comes from the squared magnitude of the total amplitude for an outcome.

Adding ordinary positive path probabilities misses this cancellation. Interference does not mean that a probability is negative. A probability remains between zero and one. The quantities that combine first are amplitudes, which are different from the final probabilities.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Equal opposite amplitudes: 1/2 + (-1/2) = 0; squared magnitude of the total is 0.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
At one output, two model path amplitudes are one half and minus one half. Their sum is zero, so that output probability is zero. Adding the separate squared values would give one half and would lose the cancellation.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

At an output, the model amplitudes are 1/2 and 1/2. Add them first, then square the result. Compare with squaring each first.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The amplitude sum is one, whose square is one. Squaring each one-half first gives one-quarter plus one-quarter, or one-half. The missing cross term explains the difference.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which order follows the quantum interference rule for indistinguishable alternatives?**

- **A.** Add amplitudes, then take squared magnitude
- **B.** Square probabilities, then subtract one
- **C.** Add only the path probabilities

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. Add amplitudes, then take squared magnitude**

Indistinguishable alternatives combine at the amplitude level. Taking the squared magnitude afterward includes the interference term that separate probability addition misses.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
These are contributions to one output. A complete physical experiment must give normalized probabilities across all outputs.
:::
