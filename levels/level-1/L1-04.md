## Learn

A conditional question first selects a group and then asks about results within it. Suppose the question asks about red objects among squares. Only the squares belong in the denominator. Circles are outside the selected group for this question.

The phrase given that marks a condition. The chance of red given square can differ from the chance of square given red. They select different groups. Read the whole question before you choose a numerator or denominator.

Conditional frequencies are useful for prediction. They can also reveal that groups differ. A difference between selected groups does not by itself explain a cause. The way the groups were selected and other shared causes can matter.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
P(red given square) = red-square count / square count, when square count is positive.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A tray has four red squares, two blue squares, and six red circles. Among squares, four of six are red, so the red fraction is 4/6. Among red objects, four of ten are squares, so that fraction is 4/10.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A club has three girls with bikes, five girls without bikes, and two boys with bikes. What fraction of the girls have bikes?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

There are eight girls, and three of them have bikes. The requested fraction is 3/8. The boys are outside the conditioning group, so they are not in this denominator.
:::

## Check your understanding

**For the fraction of blue objects among circles, which total is the denominator?**

- **A.** All blue objects
- **B.** All objects in the room
- **C.** All circles

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. All circles**

The word among restricts the group to circles. Count blue circles in the numerator and all circles in the denominator.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
A conditional frequency is an association in the selected data, not automatically an intervention effect.
:::
