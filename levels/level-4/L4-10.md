## Learn

Causal machine learning can estimate outcome regressions, treatment probabilities, or treatment effect variation. Flexible prediction methods do not create identification. Start with a defined population, intervention, outcome, and time horizon. Then state exchangeability, consistency, positivity, and any other assumptions required for the chosen target.

Transport asks whether results from one population or environment apply to another. If only the distribution of measured effect modifiers changes, valid stratum effects can sometimes be reweighted to the target distribution. This requires stable relevant mechanisms and enough source data in target strata. Unmeasured mechanism changes can defeat this strategy.

Fairness is partly a choice of social objective. Equal predictive error rates and counterfactual fairness are different criteria and can conflict. A causal fairness claim needs a justified graph and normative decisions about acceptable paths. Removing a protected attribute from inputs does not remove its information from correlated variables.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
τ(z)=E\[Y(1)-Y(0)\|Z=z\]. If these conditional effects transport and strata have support, ATEtarget=Σz τ(z)Ptarget(z). Here Z contains the stated effect modifiers.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Suppose justified effects in two strata are one and three outcome units. The source weights are one half each, giving an average effect of two. The target weights are 0.8 and 0.2. If the conditional effects transport, the target average is 0.8(1)+0.2(3)=1.4.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Conditional effects in two strata are two and minus one. Target weights are 0.25 and 0.75. Calculate the transported average under the stated stability assumptions. Does that number establish fairness?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The weighted effect is 0.25(2)+0.75(-1)=-0.25. This is a target-population causal average under the assumptions. It does not establish fairness, which needs a stated criterion and further justification.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What must come before fitting a causal effect learner?**

- **A.** A guarantee that all populations share one effect
- **B.** A choice to remove every demographic variable
- **C.** A defined causal target and justified identification assumptions

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. A defined causal target and justified identification assumptions**

A learner estimates quantities within a causal analysis. The intervention target and identification assumptions determine whether its estimates answer the intended causal question.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Predictive invariance can support causal reasoning under explicit assumptions. It is not a universal proof of mechanism stability or fairness.
:::
