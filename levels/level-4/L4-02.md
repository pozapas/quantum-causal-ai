## Learn

A directed acyclic graph, or DAG, contains directed edges and no directed cycle. Each node names a variable. An edge points from a direct cause to its child in the modeled system. A missing edge is a modeling claim, rather than proof that no relationship exists.

A path ignores edge direction while tracing connections. A noncollider on a path blocks it when conditioned on. A collider has two arrowheads meeting at that node. It blocks the path unless the collider or one of its descendants is conditioned on. Apply the rules to every path.

Two node sets are d-separated by a conditioning set when every connecting path is blocked. Under the causal Markov assumption, this graphical relation implies a conditional independence in the distribution. The reverse implication requires more assumptions, such as faithfulness. Hidden causes can require a graph with additional nodes.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
For the chain X→M→Y with no other paths, X ⟂ Y \| M under the Markov assumption. The symbol ⟂ means statistical independence.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Consider rainfall R, soil moisture M, and plant response Y in the graph R→M→Y. If this graph is complete for the question and the Markov assumption holds, conditioning on M blocks the only path from R to Y. The graph therefore implies R and Y are conditionally independent given M.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

In the graph A←Z→B, with no other edges, are A and B d-separated without conditioning? What changes when you condition on Z?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Without conditioning, the fork path is open, so A and B are not d-separated. Conditioning on the noncollider Z blocks the only path and d-separates them.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What does d-separation guarantee under the Markov assumption?**

- **A.** A conditional independence
- **B.** A nonzero causal effect
- **C.** A unique causal graph from data

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. A conditional independence**

D-separation implies the corresponding conditional independence under the Markov assumption. It does not establish a nonzero effect or identify a unique graph.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The path rules apply to the stated DAG. Missing hidden variables or selection mechanisms can change the relevant paths.
:::
