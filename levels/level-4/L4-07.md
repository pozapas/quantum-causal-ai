## Learn

Identification asks whether the target causal quantity is uniquely determined by the available distributions and assumptions. Estimation asks how to approximate an identified quantity from finite data. A larger dataset can improve estimation, but cannot resolve a causal ambiguity that remains even with exact observational probabilities.

In a fully observed causal DAG with suitable independent disturbances, an intervention removes the factors for the intervened variables from the observational factorization. This is called the truncated factorization. With hidden variables, this simple observed-variable calculation may fail. Identification then needs another justified argument or additional information.

Do-calculus provides graphical rules for exchanging observations, actions, and irrelevant terms under specified separation conditions. It is not permission to delete a do symbol whenever convenient. State the graph, target, available distributions, and assumptions first. A valid derivation must justify every transformation.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
For a fully observed Markovian DAG, P(v\\\{x\}\|do(x)) = ∏i:Vi∉X P(vi\|pa\_i), with the intervened values substituted. Here pa\_i are the parents of Vi.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Let U be a fair binary variable. Model A has X=U and Y=X. Model B has X=U and Y=U. Both give only observed pairs 00 and 11, each with probability one half. Under do(X=1), model A gives Y=1 certainly, while model B gives Y=1 with probability one half.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

For these same two models, calculate P(Y=1\|do(X=0)). Can an unlimited observational sample choose between their answers?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Model A gives probability zero because Y follows the set value of X. Model B gives probability one half because U stays fair. Their observational distributions are identical, so observations alone cannot choose between them.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What can solve an identification failure caused by observationally equivalent causal models?**

- **A.** Only increasing the same observational sample
- **B.** Removing all uncertainty intervals
- **C.** Adding justified assumptions or informative interventions

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. Adding justified assumptions or informative interventions**

Different models can agree on an exact observational distribution. New causal assumptions or relevant intervention data can distinguish their causal answers.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The truncated formula assumes the stated fully observed causal structure. It cannot simply ignore hidden common causes.
:::
