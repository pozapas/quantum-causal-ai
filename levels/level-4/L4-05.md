## Learn

Sometimes treatment and outcome have an unmeasured common cause, so ordinary backdoor adjustment is unavailable. A measured mediator can still permit identification in a special graph. The frontdoor criterion requires that the mediator intercept every directed path from treatment to outcome.

Two further path conditions are required. There must be no open backdoor path from treatment to mediator. Treatment must block every backdoor path from mediator to outcome. A measured mediator alone is insufficient. A direct treatment-to-outcome path or an unblocked mediator-outcome common cause can invalidate the ordinary formula.

First estimate the mediator distribution for each treatment. Then calculate a standardized mediator-outcome response by averaging over the observed treatment distribution. Combine these two quantities. This derivation separates the treatment-to-mediator effect from the mediator-to-outcome effect using different path conditions, rather than pretending the hidden cause was observed.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
P(y\|do(x)) = Σm P(m\|x) Σx′ P(y\|m,x′)P(x′). Here x′ is an averaging index, m is the mediator value, and the frontdoor conditions and positivity must hold.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Suppose P(X=1)=0.5. For M=1, outcome risks at X=0 and X=1 are 0.7 and 0.9, giving standardized response 0.8. For M=0, risks 0.1 and 0.3 give response 0.2. If mediator probabilities under X=1 and X=0 are 0.8 and 0.2, intervention risks are 0.68 and 0.32.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Assume a valid frontdoor graph. The standardized responses for M=1 and M=0 are 0.6 and 0.2. The probabilities of M=1 at X=1 and X=0 are 0.75 and 0.25. Find the intervention risks and difference.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

At X=1, the risk is 0.75(0.6)+0.25(0.2)=0.50. At X=0, it is 0.25(0.6)+0.75(0.2)=0.30. Subtract the second intervention risk from the first to obtain the risk difference of 0.20.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which condition is required by the ordinary frontdoor criterion?**

- **A.** The mediator may omit a directed X-to-Y path
- **B.** The mediator must intercept every directed X-to-Y path
- **C.** All hidden causes must be measured

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. The mediator must intercept every directed X-to-Y path**

The mediator must intercept every directed treatment-to-outcome path. Hidden treatment-outcome confounding can remain when the other frontdoor conditions hold.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
These conditions describe the ordinary frontdoor criterion. They are sufficient, and they are not necessary for every possible identification method.
:::
