## Learn

Causal discovery uses data and assumptions to constrain possible graphs. Constraint-based methods test conditional independences. Score-based methods compare graph scores under a model class. Functional-model methods use additional restrictions on mechanisms and noise. Every approach needs assumptions beyond a correlation matrix.

Markov-equivalent DAGs imply the same conditional independences. Without extra information, conditional independence tests can identify an equivalence class rather than one unique DAG. A collider can distinguish graphs that share a skeleton, but many edge directions remain unresolved even when all tests are exact.

Faithfulness excludes independences produced by exact parameter cancellation rather than graph separation. Finite samples can also hide weak dependencies. Continuous optimization methods such as NOTEARS impose an acyclicity constraint, but a converged objective does not remove assumptions about functional form, hidden causes, measurement, or identifiability.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
For a real weighted adjacency matrix W with d nodes, NOTEARS uses h(W)=tr(exp(W∘W))-d=0 to encode acyclicity. The symbol ∘ means elementwise multiplication.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
The graphs A→B→C and A←B←C have the same three-node chain skeleton and no collider. Both imply A and C are independent given B under the Markov assumption. Observing that independence alone therefore cannot determine which end of this chain is the first cause.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Compare A→B→C with A→B←C, assuming no hidden variables and faithful distributions. Which graph implies A and C are independent without conditioning?
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The collider graph A→B←C blocks the only A-to-C path without conditioning, so it implies marginal independence. The directed chain leaves that path open and does not imply marginal independence.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What does a DAG optimization solver returning one graph establish by itself?**

- **A.** That no hidden common cause exists
- **B.** That the graph is the unique physical truth
- **C.** That it returned a solution under its model and objective

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. That it returned a solution under its model and objective**

An optimization result is conditional on its objective and assumptions. It does not itself prove causal sufficiency or a unique physical explanation.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Discovery guarantees depend on assumptions and test accuracy. Report unresolved directions instead of forcing a single causal story.
:::
