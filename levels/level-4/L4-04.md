## Learn

The backdoor criterion gives a sufficient graphical condition for adjustment. A valid set contains no descendants of treatment and blocks every path that enters treatment through an incoming arrow. It removes noncausal paths while leaving the directed treatment-to-outcome paths needed for the total effect.

Calculate each outcome risk within a treatment and adjustment stratum. Then average those risks using the population distribution of the adjustment variables. Use the same population weights for every treatment value. Treatment-specific weights can preserve the selection difference that caused the original bias.

The formula also needs suitable data support. Positivity requires observations at each treatment value in the strata used for the target population. Consistency connects the recorded treatment with the intervention definition. Correct measurement and a sufficient causal model remain essential even when the arithmetic is exact.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
P(Y=y\|do(X=x)) = Σz P(Y=y\|X=x,Z=z)P(Z=z), when Z satisfies the backdoor criterion and the required probabilities are defined.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Let Z be zero or one with equal population probability. Risks under X = 1 are 0.2 and 0.8 in those strata. Risks under X = 0 are 0.1 and 0.6. Standardized risks are 0.5 and 0.35. The risk difference is 0.15 under the stated identification assumptions.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Keep equal population weights. Risks under X = 1 are 0.25 and 0.65. Risks under X = 0 are 0.15 and 0.35. Find both adjusted risks and their difference.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The adjusted risk under one is (0.25+0.65)/2 = 0.45. Under zero it is (0.15+0.35)/2 = 0.25. The adjusted risk difference is 0.20.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which weights appear in the ordinary backdoor formula?**

- **A.** The outcome distribution
- **B.** The population distribution P(Z)
- **C.** Only the treated group's distribution P(Z\|X=1)

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. The population distribution P(Z)**

Both intervention risks are standardized to the same target distribution P(Z). Treatment-specific weights can retain differences caused by confounding.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Adjustment cannot remove bias from an omitted common cause unless another justified identification strategy applies.
:::
