## Learn

A potential outcome describes the outcome a unit would have under a specified treatment. Write Y(1) and Y(0) for two treatment options. The individual effect is their difference. Only the potential outcome corresponding to the received treatment is usually observed for each unit.

The average treatment effect is the population average of those individual differences. Random assignment makes treatment independent of potential outcomes in the assignment distribution. Together with consistency, positivity, and an appropriate treatment and interference definition, this permits identification by a comparison of treatment groups.

Randomization does not make every realized sample perfectly balanced. It supports uncertainty calculations based on the assignment design. Missing outcomes, treatment nonadherence, or interference can change the target and the analysis. Distinguish the effect of assignment from the effect of treatment actually received.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
ATE = E\[Y(1)-Y(0)\] = E\[Y(1)\]-E\[Y(0)\]. Under random assignment and the stated assumptions, ATE = E\[Y\|X=1\]-E\[Y\|X=0\].
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Consider a model population whose mean score would be six under option one and two under option zero. Its average treatment effect is four score units. A randomized sample estimates this difference, but sampling variation can make the observed difference larger or smaller than four.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Four model units have potential outcome pairs (Y(0),Y(1)) of (1,3), (2,2), (4,7), and (3,2). Calculate the population average treatment effect.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

The individual effects are two, zero, three, and minus one. Their sum is four across four units, so the average treatment effect is one outcome unit.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What does random assignment establish in its assignment distribution?**

- **A.** Treatment is independent of potential outcomes
- **B.** Every finite sample is perfectly balanced
- **C.** Every participant receives the assigned treatment

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. Treatment is independent of potential outcomes**

Random assignment provides independence by design. Perfect realized balance and full adherence are different properties and do not follow automatically.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
A treatment with several materially different versions needs a clear definition. Interference requires outcomes indexed by more than one unit's treatment.
:::
