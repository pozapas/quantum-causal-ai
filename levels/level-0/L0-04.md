## Learn

A fair comparison makes the question clear. Suppose you ask whether a steeper ramp helps a toy car travel farther. Use the same car and floor. Change the ramp height as your planned action.

Measure distance from the same starting mark each time. Release the car without an extra push. If you change the car and the ramp together, you cannot tell which change explains a different distance.

Repeat each test because small differences can occur. A wheel can turn slightly, or a floor can have a bump. Repeated results help you see whether the planned change has a stable effect.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Comparison: same car, same floor, different ramp height.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A car travels 40 centimetres after release from a low ramp. It travels 60 centimetres from a higher ramp. You keep the car, floor, and release method the same. This is better evidence than comparing two different cars.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

You test whether water helps a plant grow. Name two conditions to keep similar and one condition to change.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Use similar plants, soil, light, and test times. Change the planned amount of water. Repeat with more than one plant because plants can differ even when they look similar.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which ramp test gives a clearer comparison?**

- **A.** Change the ramp height and keep the car
- **B.** Change the car, floor, and ramp
- **C.** Use no starting mark

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. Change the ramp height and keep the car**

Keeping the car and other conditions similar makes the ramp-height change easier to study. Repetition still helps assess variation.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Keeping visible conditions similar cannot guarantee that all other causes are controlled.
:::
