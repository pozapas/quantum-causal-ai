## Learn

Light helps us see objects and can also be the subject of an experiment. Shine a safe lamp toward a wall. Put a card in the path. You can observe a shadow where the card blocks light.

Write what you can see before you explain it. You might record a dark region on the wall and its shape. That is an observation. A model is an explanation that tries to connect the card, lamp, and shadow.

Quantum science studies how physical systems behave, including light in carefully controlled experiments. A shadow alone does not show the full quantum rules. Later lessons use interference and measurement to examine rules that simple everyday pictures cannot capture.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Test record: lamp position, card position, shadow shape.
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
Place a square card between a lamp and a wall. You observe a roughly square shadow. Move the card closer to the lamp and record the changed shadow size. Keep the lamp and wall fixed during the comparison.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

A shadow grows when you move a card. Write one observation and one question for a further fair test.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Observation: the recorded shadow became larger. A useful question is whether moving the card closer to the lamp increases shadow size while the lamp and wall stay fixed.
:::

## Check your understanding

**Which sentence is a direct observation?**

- **A.** The shadow is wider in this record
- **B.** The card has a hidden wish
- **C.** Every light experiment has this result

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**A. The shadow is wider in this record**

The first sentence describes the recorded appearance. The other sentences add an unsupported explanation or extend far beyond this test.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
Use a safe lamp. Do not look into a laser or a very bright light.
:::
