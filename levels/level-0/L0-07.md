## What you will learn

- I can explain why I cannot see things in a very dark room.
- I can test what different things do to a flashlight beam.
- I can write what I saw before I guess why.

## Story: The lost dinosaur

It is bedtime and the room is very dark. Ryan drops his toy dinosaur. He feels around on the floor, slowly. "I cannot see it anywhere!"

Parsa clicks on a flashlight. The beam lands on the dinosaur. "There it is!" says Ryan. Parsa says, "Light from the flashlight bounced off your dinosaur and into your eyes. In the dark, no light was coming from it."

Then they wonder what other things do to light. They tape white paper to the wall. They hold clear plastic, wax paper, cardboard, and a small mirror in the beam, one at a time.

When they hold up the cardboard, Ryan says, "The cardboard ate the light!" Parsa shakes his head. "Let's write what we saw first. I saw no spot on the paper." They save the why for later.

The clear plastic left a bright spot. The wax paper left a soft, dim spot. The mirror sent the spot up to the ceiling. Mirrors reflect light, so the spot bounced back toward their side of the room.

## Hands-on: Flashlight test

*About 20 minutes.*

**You need:**

- A flashlight
- A sheet of white paper and some tape
- Clear plastic, like a clear food lid or a sheet protector
- Wax paper or baking paper
- A piece of cardboard
- A small mirror, plastic if you have one
- Paper and a pencil

**Steps:**

1. Tape the white paper to a wall. Make the room dim, but not totally dark.
2. Make a chart. Write the four things down the side. Make two columns: My guess and I saw.
3. Before you test, write your guess for each thing. Will light go through?
4. Stand one big step from the wall. Shine the flashlight at the white paper. Look at the bright spot.
5. Hold each thing in the beam, one at a time. Keep the flashlight in the same spot each time.
6. Look at the paper. Is the spot bright, dim, or gone? Write what you saw.
7. For the mirror, tip it a little. Look for a spot on the wall or ceiling. Keep the spot away from faces.
8. Sort your four things into groups: lets light through, lets some light through, blocks light, bounces light back.

:::{tip} Record it
Your chart with two columns, My guess and I saw, for clear plastic, wax paper, cardboard, and the mirror. Then write each thing under one of the four groups.
:::

:::{warning} Stay safe
Never shine a flashlight or a mirror spot into anyone's eyes. Use a flashlight, never a laser pointer. Move slowly in a dim room. A grown-up should hold a glass mirror.
:::

## Practice together

Parsa holds clear plastic in the flashlight beam. What do you think he will see on the white paper?

:::{dropdown} Check your answer
A bright spot, almost as bright as with nothing in the way. Clear plastic lets most of the light through.
:::

## Try it yourself

**1.** Amin is in a dark room with a sleeping cat. Amin turns on a lamp. Now Amin can see the cat. Why?

:::{dropdown} Check your answer
Light from the lamp bounces off the cat and reaches Amin's eyes. In the dark, no light was coming from the cat.
:::

**2.** Which group would a piece of foil go in? Make a guess, then test it if you can.

:::{dropdown} Check your answer
No spot shows behind the foil. Shiny foil bounces light back, like a mirror. So it goes in bounces light back.
:::

## Talk about it

- Which result surprised you? What did you guess before you tested it?
- Why do we need light to see things?
- Ryan said the cardboard ate the light. How could you find out more about what really happens?

## Check yourself

**1. Why could Ryan not see his dinosaur in the dark room?**

- **A.** The dark covered the dinosaur like a blanket.
- **B.** No light was coming from the dinosaur to his eyes.
- **C.** The dinosaur was too small to see.

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. No light was coming from the dinosaur to his eyes.**

You see something when light from it reaches your eyes. In a very dark room, there is no light to bounce off it.
:::

**2. You hold wax paper in the beam. A soft, dim spot shows on the wall. Which group does wax paper go in?**

- **A.** Bounces light back
- **B.** Blocks light
- **C.** Lets some light through

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. Lets some light through**

Some light got to the wall, so the spot was dim but not gone. That means wax paper lets some light through.
:::

**3. Remember Lesson 3. Ryan says, "The cardboard ate the light." What should go in the I saw column?** *(Review of L0-03)*

- **A.** The cardboard is dark inside, so it hides the light.
- **B.** There was no spot on the paper behind the cardboard.
- **C.** The cardboard ate the light.

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**B. There was no spot on the paper behind the cardboard.**

Write what you saw first. Eating or hiding the light is a guess about why, so save it for later.

## Words to know

**beam**
: A long line of light, like from a flashlight.

**reflect**
: To bounce light back, like a mirror does.

:::{dropdown} For grown-ups: about this lesson
:open:

**Why it matters.** This lesson follows two grade 1 expectations: objects can be seen only when they are lit [@ngss_1ps4_2], and different materials placed in a beam of light let most of it through, let some through, block it, or send it back [@ngss_1ps4_3]. The key idea is that we see an object when light from it enters our eyes. Keeping an I saw column apart from guesses about why continues the K-2 practice of making careful observations [@ngss_appf].

**Watch for these mix-ups.**

- Our eyes send something out to see. (Light travels from the object into our eyes.)
- Dark is a thing that fills a room. (Dark just means little or no light is there.)
- Clear things let all the light through. (Most light passes, but some bounces off; that is why you can see a dim reflection in a window.)
- Things that block light do nothing to it. (They bounce some back, which is how we see them, and soak up the rest.)

**To make it easier.** Test just two things, clear plastic and cardboard, and use the words goes through and stops.

**To make it harder.** Test more things from around the house, such as tissue paper, a plastic bag, cloth, or a clear plastic cup of water, set on a towel. Try stacking two sheets of wax paper. Is the spot dimmer?

**Limits.** The four groups are a simplification. Most things do several of these at once: clear plastic also reflects a little, cardboard reflects some light (that is why we can see it), and wax paper scatters the light it lets through. The lesson describes what happens to the beam; it does not yet explain why materials differ.
