**The big question:** How do we find out what makes things happen?

Children learn to look closely, count carefully, sort, and run small fair tests with things found at home. They practice telling what they saw apart from why it happened. Every later level of the course builds on these habits.
:::

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who it is for | Children aged about 6 to 9 who can count to 20. A grown-up reads along, helps with materials, and talks through the questions. No screen time is needed for the hands-on parts. |
| Lessons | 10 lessons of about 30 minutes, plus a 2-part final project |
| Pace | Two lessons a week takes about six weeks, including the final project. Any lesson can be split into two sittings: story and activity one day, practice and checks the next. This helps most in the lessons with a 20-minute activity. |
| Total sittings | 12 |

## How a lesson works

1. Warm up: remember the last lesson in two minutes.
2. Read the story together.
3. Do the hands-on activity and record what happens.
4. Practice together, then try two on your own.
5. Talk about it, then answer the three check questions. Say your answer out loud before you open a dropdown.
6. Grown-ups: read the short guide at the end of the page.

## Final project: My own fair test

Over two sittings, the child picks a question, plans a fair test, runs it three times, and shares what they found.

**Pick one question, or make up your own:**

- Does a toy car roll farther on a smooth floor or on carpet?
- Which paper towel soaks up more water, a thick one or a thin one?
- Does an ice cube melt faster on a plate in a sunny spot or in the shade?

**Steps:**

1. Pick one question.
2. Say what you will change. Say what you will keep the same.
3. Guess what will happen and why.
4. Do the test three times for each choice. Write every result.
5. Draw your results as a picture graph or a bar graph, with one square for each tile or step.
6. Tell someone what you found, and one thing you are still not sure about.

:::{warning} Project safety
Roll balls and cars away from stairs. Wipe up water right away so no one slips. Never look straight at the sun.
:::

**How it is checked.** Each part has three steps up. Celebrate every step.

| Part | Getting started | Got it | Can explain it |
|---|---|---|---|
| My question | I picked a question with help. | I picked a question I can test. | I can say why the question matters to me. |
| My fair plan | I changed one thing, with help. | I changed one thing and kept the rest the same. | I can say what could make the test unfair. |
| My record | I wrote some results. | I wrote three results for each choice. | I can point to results that did not match and say why that is fine. |
| What I found | I said what happened. | I said what happened and used my results. | I said one thing I am still not sure about. |

## Materials for the whole unit

- Small things to count and sort: buttons, blocks, dry pasta, or toy pieces (about 30), and a few bowls
- Paper, pencils, crayons or markers, and grid paper if you have it
- Sticky notes, small paper cards, and masking tape
- A toy car or a ball that rolls, two hardcover books, and a flat board or stiff cardboard
- An ice cube, a plate, and a clock or timer
- One coin and a paper cup
- Six matching paper or plastic cups and a ruler with centimeters
- A flashlight, clear plastic, wax paper, cardboard, white paper, and a small mirror (plastic if you have one)
- Four playing cards or paper squares
- For the final project: a ball or toy car, paper towels and a cup of water, or an ice cube and a plate

:::{warning} Safety
Choose activities that fit the child. Keep small parts and coins away from babies and toddlers. Never point a flashlight or a mirror spot into anyone's eyes, and never look straight at the sun. A grown-up holds anything glass and does the cutting. Keep water away from screens and plugs, and keep activities away from stairs.
:::

## How progress is checked

Progress shows up three ways. Each lesson ends with three check questions. From Lesson 2 on, one of them looks back at an earlier lesson. Each hands-on activity produces a record (a tally, a table, or a drawing) that a grown-up can look at. The final project uses a four-part checklist.

:::{dropdown} For grown-ups: how this unit is designed
:open:

The unit was planned backward: first the eight outcomes, then the evidence that shows them, then the lessons [@ubd_whitepaper]. Each lesson moves from remembering what the child knows, to watching an example, to doing, to talking it through [@merrill2002]. Short check questions and a warm-up that looks back are built in on purpose, because practice testing and spaced practice are among the best-supported ways to learn [@dunlosky2013]. Young children already reason about causes from what they see [@gopnik2000; @gopnik2004]; this unit gives that instinct words and a method. Options for different learners follow the Universal Design for Learning guidelines [@udl]. When the child answers a question, let them say their answer before opening it, then open it together and praise the thinking as well as the right answer.

**Ways to adapt it.**

- Every story can be read aloud. Pictures and real objects come before symbols.
- Children can show what they know by talking, drawing, pointing, or writing.
- Each lesson has a way to make it easier and a way to make it harder.
- Activities use cheap household things, so every family can take part.
