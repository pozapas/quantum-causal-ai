## Learn

A clue can help you predict an event. Dark clouds can help you predict rain. Seeing many umbrellas can also be a clue that rain is present. A useful clue need not be a control switch for the event.

Opening an umbrella does not normally make rain start. Rain can make people open umbrellas instead. To ask about a cause, ask what would change if you deliberately changed the possible cause under suitable conditions.

A prediction system can learn that umbrellas and rain occur together. A causal system must ask a further question about actions and results. Good decisions need that distinction because acting on a clue may not change the thing you want.

:::{important} Key relation
:class: qcai-equation
Clue question: will it rain? Action question: what happens if I open an umbrella?
:::

:::{tip} Worked example
A child sees wet roads on rainy days and predicts rain from a wet road. A water truck can also wet a road. Making the road wet does not create rain, even though wet roads were useful clues.
:::

## Practice

:::{admonition} Exercise
:class: tip

Ice cream sales and swimming visits both rise on hot days. Would buying ice cream necessarily cause a person to swim? Name a possible shared cause.
:::

:::{dropdown} Solution

Buying ice cream does not necessarily cause swimming. Hot weather can increase both activities. It is a possible shared cause that must be considered before a causal claim.
:::

## Check your understanding

**What can make two events occur together without one causing the other?**

- **A.** A spelling change
- **B.** The lack of a record
- **C.** A shared cause

:::{dropdown} Show the answer
**C. A shared cause**

A shared cause can affect both events. Their association can then help prediction without showing that changing one changes the other.
:::

:::{caution} Scope and assumptions
The weather examples teach a distinction. They are not complete physical or social models.
:::
